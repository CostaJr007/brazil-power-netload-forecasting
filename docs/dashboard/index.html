Benchmark (what a desk already gets for free): D+1 = ONS day-ahead schedule (DESSEM) corrected by its own mean error over the last 28 days at the same hour; against the raw schedule the D+1 gain roughly doubles, but most of that is the bias correction. D+2 = previous-day ONS schedule bias-corrected by hour and weekday (no same-day schedule exists yet). Recipes were selected on 2025 and frozen; 2026 is out of sample but was also used for later diagnostics, so treat it as validation. MAE = mean absolute error of hourly net load; this view uses every available hour.

What we learnedDuring the Nov 3–7 heat wave the operator's bias flipped sign; the 28-day bias correction kept removing the old bias and D+1 lost to the operator. D+2 still won by about 20%. Nothing was retuned: adjusting to one bad week would be overfitting.

Each forecast was rebuilt with data cut at 10 pm the night before, archived weather and frozen recipes. This is a replay, not a true as-of run: inputs are today's revised ONS values cut by date. Actuals are today's ONS balance; the most recent day is still preliminary.
